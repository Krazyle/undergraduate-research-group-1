## Population of the Philippines in 2026
Data from the United Nations indicates that the population of the Philippines stood at 117 million in October 2025.
The same dataset also shows that the Philippines’ population increased by 942 thousand (+0.8 percent) between the end of 2024 and the end of 2025.
Meanwhile, in late 2025, 49.1 percent of the Philippines’ population lived in urban centres, whereas 50.9 percent lived in rural areas.
At that time, 50.1 percent of the Philippines’ population was female, while 49.9 percent of the population was male.

Data from [GSMA Intelligence](https://www.gsmaintelligence.com/?utm_source=kepios&utm_medium=partner) shows that there were 137 million cellular mobile connections in the Philippines at the end of 2025.
For perspective, many people make use of more than one mobile connection, so it’s not unusual for mobile connection figures to significantly exceed figures for total population.
For example, the same person might have one mobile connection for personal use, but also use a separate mobile connection for work activities. The rise of [eSIMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESIM) has made this even easier over recent years.
Indeed, GSMA Intelligence’s numbers indicate that mobile connections in the Philippines were equivalent to 117 percent of the total population in October 2025.
Looking at trends over time, the number of mobile connections in the Philippines increased by 4.0 million (+3.0 percent) between the end of 2024 and the end of 2025.
Meanwhile, GSMA Intelligence’s data suggests that 89.0 percent of mobile connections in the Philippines can now be considered “broadband”, which means that they connect via 3G, 4G, or 5G mobile networks.
However, devices that connect to “broadband” mobile networks do not necessarily use cellular mobile data — for example, some subscription plans may only include access to voice and SMS services — so this broadband figure should not be considered a proxy for mobile internet use.

## YouTube users in the Philippines in 2026
Google’s advertising resources indicate that YouTube had 59.6 million users in the Philippines in late 2025.
It’s important to stress that these advertising reach figures do not necessarily represent the same thing as monthly active user figures though, and there may be meaningful differences between the size of YouTube’s ad audience and its total active user base.
Caveats aside, the company’s own data suggests that YouTube’s ad reach in late 2025 was equivalent to 50.9 percent of the Philippines’ total population at the end of the year.
To put those figures in perspective, YouTube ads reached 60.8 percent of the Philippines’ total internet user base (regardless of age) in October 2025.
At that time, 51.2 percent of YouTube’s adult ad audience in the Philippines was female, while 48.8 percent was male.
Note: at the time of report production, Google’s advertising resources only published audience gender data for “female” and “male” users.

Data published in Google’s own ad planning tools show that YouTube’s potential ad reach in the Philippines increased by 1.90 million (+3.3 percent) between the end of 2024 and late 2025.

However, it’s worth noting that Google only publishes updates to its figures for YouTube reach every three to six months, and actual reach may have evolved since this data was published in Google’s ad planning tools.

In late 2025, the company’s own data indicated that 53.7 percent of X’s adult ad audience in the Philippines was female, while 46.2 percent was male.
However, X appears to infer its users’ gender, by analysing signals such as the name that users enter in their profile, and their broader activity on the platform.
This contrasts with the gender data offered in the advertising tools of platforms like Facebook, which tend to rely on gender details that users themselves enter in their own profile.
Moreover, our analysis suggests that X’s inferences may be less representative of users’ actual gender in countries where English is not the dominant language.
For example, consumer research findings published by [GWI](https://www.gwi.com/?utm_campaign=17055411-FY26_ALL_GL_Kepios_Simon_Kemp&utm_source=simon_kemp) often offer a different picture of X use by gender compared with X’s own audience data.
Having said that, the apparent gender “skew” that frequently appears in X’s inferred gender data may also be due in part to the high number of legitimate but “non-human” accounts that feature in X’s active user data, including accounts that represent businesses and brands, animals, music groups, and various other entities [note that X doesn’t currently distinguish between “people” and other types of account in the ways that platforms like Facebook and Instagram do].
Regardless of the cause though, we’d advise caution when analysing or interpreting X’s reported figures for use by gender.

Moreover, we’ve identified some unusual trends in the data reported in X’s advertising tools in recent months, so we’d advise caution when it comes to analysing changes over time in these X reach figures.
