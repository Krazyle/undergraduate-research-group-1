## M3. Chapter 2: Review of Related Literature & Studies

A Guide to Topical Taxonomy, Algorithmic Benchmarking, Synthesizing Literature & CEU Manuscript Protocols

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, Computer Science research students will be able to:

* Shift from legacy geographical reviews (Local vs. Foreign) to Topical / Thematic Structuring based on computational paradigms and algorithmic methods.

* Conduct systematic literature searches in high-impact CS repositories (IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, Springer, ScienceDirect) within a 3 to 5-year recency window.

* Evaluate existing empirical studies based on computational performance metrics (latency, memory footprint, accuracy, throughput, and hardware efficiency).

* Construct an Algorithmic Literature Taxonomy Matrix to map state-of-the-art approaches against current limitations.

* Draft a rigorous Synthesis of the Related Literature at the end of the chapter to explicitly justify the proposed project's research gap.

## 1. Paradigm Shift: Topical vs. Geographical RRL in CS

In traditional academic writing, literature was often segregated into "Foreign Literature," "Local Literature," "Foreign Studies," and "Local Studies." In CEU Computer Science research, this geographical separation is deprecated.

Why CS Literature is Topical: Computer Science algorithms, neural architectures, distributed protocols, and mathematical principles operate globally without geographic boundaries. A quantization technique developed in Asia functions identically on hardware in Europe or North America.

## 3. Literature Sourcing & Critical Evaluation Protocol

Computer Science rapidly evolves. Literature that is more than 5 years old may review obsolete frameworks or outdated hardware benchmarks. Adhere strictly to these CEU searching protocols:

## ⇓ Database & Search String Protocol

* Approved Sources: IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, arXiv (peer-reviewed/accepted papers only).

* Search Strategy: Use advanced Boolean query strings: ("model quantization" OR "pruning") AND ("edge device" OR "Raspberry Pi") AND "latency".

* Citation Manager: Utilize Zotero or Mendeley for automatic APA 7th Edition indexing.

## ⇓ Recency & Empirical Focus

* Recency Window: At least 80% of cited papers must be published within the last 3 to 5 years.

* Empirical Rigor: Prioritize studies that publish explicit benchmarking metrics (FPS, RAM overhead, BLEU/F1 scores, energy consumption).

* No Blog Posts/Tutorials: Medium, Medium/TowardsDataScience,GeeksforGeeks, and personal GitHub READMEs are not valid scientific literature.

## 5. Drafting the Synthesis of the Related Literature

The final section of Chapter 2 is the Synthesis of the Related Literature and Studies. This is not merely a summary paragraph; it is a critical, analytical conclusion (1 to 2 pages) that ties all cited literature together to justify your research.

## The 3-Step Formula for a Strong CS Synthesis

Step 1: The Commonalities & Trends (What is established?)
Summarize the overarching methodologies, frameworks, and tools currently accepted in the field based on the reviewed studies. Point out common techniques that authors have successfully deployed.

Step 2: The Critical Gap & Bottleneck (What is missing?)
Highlight the recurring limitations, trade-offs, or unaddressed conditions in existing literature. Show where existing algorithms fail (e.g., high execution overhead, lack of optimization for specific constrained hardware, accuracy drops under dynamic real-world environments).

Step 3: The Research Bridge & Contribution (How does your study fill the gap?)
Explicitly position your proposed study as the solution to the identified gaps. Detail how your chosen methodology, mathematical model, or artifact design builds upon—yet distinctively improves—prior work.
