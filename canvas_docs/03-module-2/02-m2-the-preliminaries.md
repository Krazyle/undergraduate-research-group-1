# M2. Lesson 1: Structuring the Research Proposal & Academic CS Writing Style

Transforming an Academic Idea into a Technical Blueprint for Computing Systems

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this foundational module, Computer Science research students will be able to:

- Understand the function of a research proposal as a binding technical blueprint.
- Formulate precise, objective, and descriptive CS research titles avoiding commercial/marketing fluff.
- Construct a highly structured 5-pillar micro-snapshot Abstract.
- Master the formal **Academic Research Writing Style** specific to Computer Science (scholarly tone, third-person perspective, exact computational terminology, and mathematical variable handling).

## 1. The Proposal as a Technical Blueprint

In Computer Science and software engineering, a research proposal is not merely an essay or a general statement of intent. It is a **binding technical blueprint**. If approved, it serves as the exact technical specifications and roadmap you are expected to execute during your development and evaluation phases.

**The Engineering Mindset:** If an external developer reads your proposal, they should be able to reconstruct your experimental pipelines, data pre-processing steps, and system architecture without asking for clarification.

## 2. Anatomy of a CS Research Proposal

A rigorous computing proposal follows a structured, standard breakdown where each section serves a specific technical function:

### Part 1: The Title

Your title must be descriptive, objective, and precise. Avoid flashy, marketing-style prose (e.g., *"A Revolution in Web Applications"* or *"An Amazing App for Farmers"*). Instead, clearly define the **core technology/algorithm**, your **method/approach**, and the **application domain**.

**Example:** *"An Optimized YOLOv8 Architecture for Real-Time Edge Detection of Urban Traffic Anomalies"*

### Part 2: The Abstract (The Micro-Snapshot)

A highly structured, single-paragraph summary (typically 150–250 words) that provides an immediate overview of the entire project. It must clearly outline these **five foundational pillars**:

**1. Context:** The broader technical landscape or field of study.

**2. Problem:** The specific technical bottleneck, computational constraint, or research gap.

**3. Proposed Solution:** Your system, modified algorithm, or methodology designed to address the gap.

**4. Methodology:** How you will implement, execute, and test your solution.

**5. Expected Contribution:** The tangible value, empirical baseline, or performance improvement your work will deliver.

#### Chapter 1: Introduction

Establishes key domain background, states the problem statements clearly, defines technical scope/limitations, and lists explicit, measurable research objectives.

#### Chapter 2: Literature Review

Synthesizes and critiques existing works topically rather than listing them geographically. Argues why state-of-the-art solutions are insufficient to justify your approach.

#### Chapter 3: Methodology

The explicit technical implementation design. Details system architectures, algorithms, datasets, UML diagrams, hardware testbeds, and performance metrics.

## 3. Academic Writing Style Rules for Computer Science

Writing a technical manuscript requires a shift from conversational prose to **formal academic research writing**. Adhere strictly to these style principles across all chapters:

#### Rule A: Maintain Academic Objectivity & Eliminate First-Person Pronouns

Scientific writing focuses on the *research and the artifact*, not the researcher. Avoid personal pronouns (**"I", "we", "my team", "our study"**).

- **Incorrect (✗):** *"In Chapter 3, I will implement a neural network to classify leaf images."*
- **Correct (✓):** *"In Chapter 3, a convolutional neural network is developed to classify leaf images."*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Rule B: Avoid Commercial Hype, Fluff, and Subjective Claims

Computer Science research is grounded in empirical data. Never claim a system is "best," "fastest," "seamless," or "revolutionary" unless supported by statistical benchmarks.

- **Incorrect (✗):** *"Our amazing system provides super-fast and perfect plant detection for local farmers."*
- **Correct (✓):** *"The proposed dynamic quantized model achieves an average inference latency of 24ms with an F1-score of 0.91."*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Rule C: Technical Precision vs. Colloquial Language

Use precise computer science terminology instead of generic terms.

| Informal / Vague Term (✗) | Precise CS Terminology (✓) |
|----|----|
| "The app handles a lot of data quickly." | "The system processes high-throughput data streams with low latency." |
| "We fixed the code so it doesn't break." | "Fault-tolerance mechanisms were integrated to maintain execution stability." |
| "The algorithm makes guessing better." | "The algorithm reduces classification loss and optimizes prediction accuracy." |

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Rule D: Proper Formatting for Math, Equations, and Variables

Whenever introducing mathematical formulas, algorithms, or metrics, variables must be formatted consistently. Always define every variable immediately following an equation (e.g., *where \$t\_{\text{exec}}\$ represents execution time in milliseconds and \$N\$ represents total frame count*).

**Key Takeaway for CS Researchers:**\
Treat your research proposal as an engineering document. Clear structure, rigorous writing style, precise definitions, and empirical metrics transform an abstract concept into an actionable scientific contribution.
